Genetics and evolution of drought tolerance

Plants have evolved very different ways of coping with drought, from tolerating it to avoiding it altogether by dropping their leaves. We study the genetic basis of these strategies within a species, and their evolutionary history across tropical trees.

Projects

  • Genetic basis of drought tolerance in black cottonwood
  • Evolution of drought deciduousness in tropical trees

Genetic basis of drought tolerance in black cottonwood

What genes underlie water use efficiency and xylem drought tolerance in black cottonwood (Populus trichocarpa), a riparian tree of the Pacific Northwest? We phenotype hydraulic and photosynthetic traits and drought responses to identify genes associated with drought adaptation.

Collaborators: Posy Busby, Jared LeBoldus, Chris Still, Margaret Krause

Evolution of drought deciduousness in tropical trees

How did drought deciduousness, shedding leaves to survive the dry season, evolve in tropical trees? This question is part of VERDE, an NSF-funded working group.

Funding: NSF (VERDE working group)

Collaborators: Catherine Hulshof, Jennifer S. Powers, Loren Albert, Toby Pennington, Natalia Umaña, and others
